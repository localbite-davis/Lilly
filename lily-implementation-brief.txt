----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: WHAT WE ARE BUILDING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Lily is a voice AI companion accessible via a standard phone call —
no app, no internet, no data plan required on the caller's side.

She serves pregnant women and new mothers in US maternity deserts
(counties with no OB hospital, no OB, and no birth center) from
whenever they first call through 12 months postpartum.

She is simultaneously:
  - A medical-grade triage system with a deterministic rules engine
  - A knowledgeable companion who gives real, practical help
  - A memory keeper who builds a living picture of each patient
  - A care coordinator who warm-handoffs to doctors and emergency services
  - A proactive check-in system that reaches out at high-risk windows

The fundamental design principle: Lily is an aunt who happens to have
medical expertise. The medical expertise does not make her cold or
clinical. The warmth does not make her medically vague. Both are always
present. Memory is the thread that ties them together.


----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: CORE DESIGN DECISIONS (READ BEFORE CODING ANYTHING)
----------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 PHONE CALL, NOT APP
  - Lily is accessed via a Twilio phone number
  - The caller needs zero data, zero apps, zero internet
  - Internet lives on our servers, not on her phone
  - Latency budget: full STT → LLM → TTS loop under 800ms
    or the call feels broken. This is the single highest risk.
    Derisk it first. Get a working voice loop before any other feature.

2.2 CALLER ID AS PRIMARY KEY
  - When a call arrives, the first action is always a caller ID lookup
  - If found: load full context, greet by name, proceed
  - If not found: offer registration or verbal login flow

2.3 VERBAL LOGIN FOR UNKNOWN NUMBERS
  - Maria may call from a borrowed phone, sister's phone, dead battery
  - Lily asks: name + due date or baby's birthday
  - Match against records. Confirm with one low-stakes detail.
  - If no match: treat as new caller, offer registration

2.4 REGISTRATION IS CONVERSATIONAL, NOT A FORM
  - No pre-registration required. Maria can dial cold.
  - Lily collects intake data through natural conversation
  - Minimum viable intake: name, due date or baby DOB, language
    preference, callback number, emergency contact name + number,
    and whether she has a home BP cuff
  - Consent is explicit and spoken. Lily explains what is stored,
    what is shared (only with treating doctors and emergency services
    on active cases), and what is never shared (insurance, ICE, police,
    custody). Maria verbally confirms.

2.5 MEMORY IS THE PRODUCT
  - Every call enriches Maria's record
  - Lily never asks the same question twice if she can help it
  - Memory includes: medical facts, life context, emotional state
    trends, support system changes, appointment history, what
    doctors have said, what standing orders exist, what equipment
    she has at home, what has and hasn't worked for her
  - When Lily references past context it feels like a friend
    remembering, not a system retrieving. Use natural language:
    "Last week you mentioned..." not "According to your record..."

2.6 THE LINE LILY DOES NOT CROSS
  - Lily CAN: comfort coach, educate, navigate resources, validate,
    listen, give evidence-based self-care suggestions, explain
    medical information in plain language, activate standing orders
    a doctor has explicitly written for this patient, surface
    past doctor recommendations
  - Lily CANNOT: diagnose, prescribe anything new, adjust existing
    medication dosages, tell Maria not to seek care when she is
    asking whether she should, make the final triage classification
    on a borderline case (that belongs to the rules engine + doctor)
  - Rule of thumb: anything a well-informed doula, a knowledgeable
    aunt, or a nurse-line script would clear in 10 seconds is fine.
    Anything requiring a medical license is not.

2.7 THE RULES ENGINE IS DETERMINISTIC
  - The LLM owns the conversation. The rules engine owns the
    final tier classification.
  - This is non-negotiable. It makes the system auditable, defensible,
    and trustworthy to clinician judges and real clinicians alike.
  - The rules engine accepts BOTH numerical inputs (when available)
    AND symptom-cluster inputs (when no numbers). Either can
    independently trigger escalation.
  - If the rules engine fires on HIGH, no LLM output overrides it.
    The call goes to emergency services. Period.
  - Rules are grounded in ACOG's Urgent Maternal Warning Signs
    (publicly available, cite them by name in the pitch)

2.8 DOCTOR REVIEW IS SYSTEM-MANAGED, NOT PATIENT-MANAGED
  - When a case is hand-up, Maria waits. She does not call back.
    She does not do anything. The system owns the timer and the
    auto-escalation. Putting burden on a stressed patient is a
    design failure.
  - 20-minute SLA for doctor response
  - If no response: auto-escalate to hand-off (emergency tier),
    system calls Maria back with instructions and ER address

2.9 LILY STAYS ON THE LINE DURING EMERGENCIES
  - High-tier calls do not end with "call 911"
  - Lily stays with Maria, conferences in 911, delivers the SBAR,
    contacts her emergency contact, calls ahead to L&D,
    and coaches Maria through what to say to paramedics
  - This is the detail that makes the project human

2.10 THE YEAR-LONG COMPANION RELATIONSHIP
  - Lily does not disappear after the birth
  - Proactive check-ins are scheduled at: first week postpartum,
    2 weeks (most missed warning signs surface here), 6 weeks,
    3 months, 6 months, 9 months, 12 months
  - Maria can also call anytime
  - EPDS postpartum depression screening is woven into postpartum
    check-ins conversationally, not as a survey read-aloud
  - The relationship built during low-stakes calls is what makes
    Maria pick up the phone when something is actually wrong


----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3: LILY'S CAPABILITIES (THE FULL SURFACE AREA)
----------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 PHYSICAL COMFORT COACHING (in-call, real-time)
  - Breathing techniques: 4-7-8, box breathing, paced breathing
    for early contractions
  - Position guidance for back pain, hip pain, swelling
    (left-side lie, pillow placement, etc.)
  - Grounding techniques for anxiety and panic: 5-4-3-2-1 sensory
  - Hydration and nutrition prompting
  - Gentle movement coaching: pelvic tilts, cat-cow, stretching
  - Warm/cold compress guidance
  - Nipple care, latch troubleshooting (breastfeeding is a massive
    unmet need — doulas charge $200/hr for this)
  - Newborn soothing techniques: 5 S's, swaddling, paced feeding

3.2 EMOTIONAL SUPPORT
  - Active listening without redirecting to solutions prematurely
  - Normalizing common pregnancy and postpartum experiences
  - Distinguishing baby blues from PPD in conversation
  - Helping Maria articulate what she actually needs
  - Coaching hard conversations (partner, family, employer)
  - Flagging when emotional state crosses into clinical territory
    and routing appropriately, never making Maria feel surveilled

3.3 EDUCATION ON DEMAND
  - Plain-language explanation of any pregnancy/postpartum term
  - What to expect at each type of appointment
  - What her test results mean (in general terms, not diagnosis)
  - Normal vs. concerning symptom ranges for each gestational stage
  - Medication safety questions (general: "is Tylenol ok while
    breastfeeding" — not dosage adjustment for existing conditions)
  - Newborn care basics: sleep, feeding, umbilical cord, jaundice

3.4 LOGISTICAL AND NAVIGATIONAL HELP
  - WIC enrollment and office locations
  - Free breast pump through insurance (most plans cover 100%)
  - Medicaid pregnancy coverage extension
  - FMLA paperwork guidance
  - SNAP / food assistance
  - Local doula collectives and maternal support groups
  - Medicaid non-emergency medical transportation (huge — most
    patients in maternity deserts don't know this exists)
  - Baby supply closets and donation programs
  - Appointment prep: what questions to ask
  - Appointment debrief: what did they say, anything unclear

3.5 STANDING ORDER ACTIVATION
  - If Dr. Chen previously wrote: "patient may use X for Y situation"
    and that situation is present, Lily surfaces it:
    "Dr. Chen cleared Unisom for your nausea back in May — 
    want to try that first?"
  - Lily NEVER invents a protocol. She surfaces existing authorization.
  - If no standing order exists for the situation: hand-up tier.
  - Doctors write standing orders via the dashboard during quiet
    moments, not only in response to active cases.
    This is a key dashboard feature.

3.6 MEMORY AS ACTIVE CARE
  - "Last week you mentioned the baby was kicking less in the
    evenings — has that gotten better or worse?"
  - "You told me your mom was flying in Tuesday — is she there?"
  - "Three weeks ago you said you were dreading the postpartum
    visit — how did it go?"
  - "You haven't mentioned sleep in a while. How is it?"
  - This is not a feature. This is the product.
    Everything else enables this.

SECTION 4: THE THREE TIERS (SOLIDIFIED)
----------------------------------------------------------------

NOTE: The LLM gathers information conversationally. The rules engine
classifies. Never the other way around.

----
TIER 1: HANDLE (Lily manages autonomously)
----

TRIGGERS (all of the following must be true):
  - No ACOG Urgent Maternal Warning Signs present
  - BP within normal range IF a reading is available
    (< 140 systolic AND < 90 diastolic)
  - No severe symptom cluster (see HIGH tier definition)
  - Mood/emotional concern within normal new-parent range
  - Case involves common pregnancy/postpartum discomfort:
    nausea, fatigue, mild swelling at end of day, Braxton Hicks,
    sleep disruption, breastfeeding difficulty, mild anxiety,
    general questions, logistical help

WHAT LILY DOES:
  - Provides comfort coaching, education, navigation as appropriate
  - Activates relevant standing orders if they exist and apply
  - Logs full conversation to Maria's record
  - Schedules follow-up SMS for next morning: "How are you feeling?"
  - Tells Maria when to call back: "If the headache gets worse or
    you start seeing spots, call me right away."

WHAT LILY DOES NOT DO:
  - Prescribe or recommend anything new
  - Diagnose
  - Dismiss symptoms she logged — they inform future calls

----
TIER 2: HAND-UP (Voluntary doctor review required)
----

TRIGGERS (any one of the following is sufficient):
  Numerical:
  - BP 140-159 systolic OR 90-109 diastolic (self-reported)
  - HR > 120 if reported by wearable or self-report with symptoms
  - SpO2 < 94% if reported by wearable
  
  Symptom cluster (no numbers required):
  - 2 or more ACOG Urgent Warning Signs without severe features
    Warning signs include: headache, visual changes, swelling
    (especially face/hands), nausea/vomiting new in third trimester,
    epigastric pain, decreased fetal movement (not at term),
    dizziness without syncope
  
  Mental health:
  - EPDS score 10-12 (moderate range) if screening is included
  - Expressed inability to cope, persistent intrusive thoughts,
    described isolation or feeling unsafe (without active suicidality)
  
  Uncertainty:
  - Lily cannot confidently classify the case with available info
  - Maria's presentation is atypical or contradictory

WHAT LILY DOES:
  1. Tells Maria calmly what is happening and the timeline:
     "I want a doctor to take a quick look at this. I'll have them
     review your case and call you back within 20 minutes. Can I
     reach you on this number?"
  2. Ends the call gracefully.
  3. Sends Maria an SMS immediately:
     - Brief summary of what was discussed
     - "A doctor is reviewing your case. I'll call you back by [time]."
     - "If you don't hear from us by [time+5min] or anything gets
       worse, please go to the nearest ER: [address]."
  4. Builds and pushes a structured case packet to the doctor queue:
     - Patient name, gestational/postpartum stage
     - All vitals reported (numerical and symptom-based)
     - ACOG warning signs present (with timestamps)
     - Lily's tentative assessment
     - Specific question for the doctor (forced-choice format):
       "BP 148/94, headache 2hrs, new edema at 32 weeks, no severe
       features. Should this go to L&D tonight or same-day tomorrow?"
     - 3 buttons: [Approve Lily's rec] [Escalate now] [Add note]
  5. Starts a 20-minute server-side countdown.

AUTO-ESCALATION (system-managed, not patient-managed):
  - If no doctor claims + responds within 20 minutes:
  - System fires: Lily calls Maria back
  - "I wasn't able to reach a doctor in time. To be safe,
    please head to the nearest ER now. I'm texting you the
    address and calling ahead to let them know you're coming."
  - System calls the ER, delivers SBAR, texts emergency contact

DOCTOR RESPONDS:
  - Lily calls Maria back within 2 minutes of doctor response
  - Delivers decision in her own voice, in Maria's language
  - Conferences in facility if escalation is recommended
  - Texts Maria: her own visit summary, doctor's note, address
  - Texts emergency contact if escalation

----
TIER 3: HAND-OFF (Emergency services activated)
----

TRIGGERS (any one of the following is sufficient):
  Numerical:
  - BP >= 160 systolic OR >= 110 diastolic

  Severe symptom cluster:
  - Visual changes (spots, blurring, loss) + headache (any severity)
  - Chest pain or shortness of breath
  - Seizure or loss of consciousness (caller reporting for Maria)
  - Heavy vaginal bleeding (soaking more than 1 pad/hour)
  - Decreased fetal movement at or after 36 weeks
  - Signs of stroke: face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty
  - Severe epigastric pain (especially right upper quadrant)

  Mental health emergency:
  - Active suicidal ideation with plan or intent
  - Expressed immediate danger to self or baby

  Uncertainty override:
  - If Lily cannot classify AND Maria sounds in acute distress:
    escalate. Never err toward handle when in doubt.

WHAT LILY DOES:
  1. Does not pause to verify. Acts immediately.
  2. Tells Maria calmly and directly what is happening:
     "Maria, what you're describing needs emergency care right now.
     I'm going to stay on the line with you while we get you help.
     Don't hang up."
  3. Initiates a three-way call with 911.
  4. When dispatch picks up, Lily delivers a structured SBAR:
     "I'm calling on behalf of [name], [gestational stage] pregnant,
     at [address on file]. She is reporting [symptoms]. Please dispatch."
  5. While on hold or awaiting dispatch: stays with Maria.
     Keeps her calm. Does not give medical advice.
     Asks her to unlock the front door. Tells her what to say
     to paramedics: "Tell them your blood pressure has been high
     and you have a severe headache."
  6. In parallel (server-side, automatic):
     - Texts Maria's emergency contact: name, what's happening, address
     - Identifies nearest L&D, calls intake, delivers SBAR:
       "32-week patient incoming with suspected hypertensive emergency,
       ETA approximately [X] minutes."
     - Sends Maria's record summary to the receiving facility
  7. Stays on the line until 911 confirms dispatch or EMTs arrive.
  8. Next morning: Lily calls to follow up.

WHAT LILY DOES NOT DO AT THIS TIER:
  - Give specific medical advice ("take this medication")
  - Allow the LLM to second-guess the rules engine classification
  - End the call and leave Maria alone

SECTION 5: FULL CALL WORKFLOW (CHRONOLOGICAL)
----------------------------------------------------------------

--- CALL ARRIVES ---

Step 1: Twilio receives call. Server fires immediately.
Step 2: Caller ID lookup against patient database.

  IF FOUND:
    - Load full patient context (name, gestational stage, medical
      history, past conversations, standing orders, equipment,
      life context, last conversation summary)
    - Lily answers: "Hi [name], this is Lily. I have you here —
      you're [X] weeks along / [baby] is [X] weeks old. What's
      going on?"
    - Proceed to CONVERSATION LOOP

  IF NOT FOUND:
    - Lily answers generically: "Hi, this is Lily — a free service
      for pregnant moms and new mothers. Have we talked before?"
    
    IF YES (returning caller from new number):
      - Verbal login: name + due date or baby's birthday
      - Search records. If match found, confirm with low-stakes
        detail, load context, proceed.
      - If no match: treat as new caller.
    
    IF NO (new caller):
      - Proceed to REGISTRATION FLOW

--- REGISTRATION FLOW ---

Lily explains what she is in two sentences and asks for consent:
"I'm a free service that helps pregnant moms and new mothers get
medical guidance, comfort, and support over the phone. Anything
you tell me stays with you, your doctors if there's an emergency,
and whoever you name as your emergency contact. We never share with
insurance, immigration, or anyone else. Is it okay if I remember
our conversations so I can help you better over time?"

IF NO: Lily can still help this call anonymously, but cannot
remember for next time. Explain this clearly. Offer again at
the end of the call.

IF YES: Collect intake conversationally:
  - First name (and nickname if she prefers)
  - Are you currently pregnant or have you recently given birth?
  - Due date (if pregnant) or baby's birthday (if postpartum)
  - Language preference
  - Best callback number
  - Emergency contact: name and number
  - Do you have a home blood pressure cuff?
  - Do you have any wearable device that tracks heart rate?

Save record. Confirm back to Maria in one sentence.
Transition directly into: "So what's going on today?"

--- CONVERSATION LOOP ---

The loop is: Maria speaks → STT transcribes → LLM processes →
tool calls fire (memory write, symptom log, vitals log, etc.) →
LLM generates response → TTS speaks → Maria hears.

All under 800ms end-to-end. This is the hard technical constraint.

The LLM's job during conversation:
  a) Be Lily: warm, knowledgeable, remembering, unhurried
  b) Work through the relevant clinical questions naturally
     (not as a checklist — as a friend who happens to know medicine)
  c) Call tools to log everything structured in the background
  d) Ask the BP question when relevant: "Do you have your cuff
     handy? Can you take a reading? I'll wait."
  e) Ask about wearable data if she has one: "What does your
     [watch/device] say your heart rate is right now?"
  f) Check for incoming vitals SMS if wearable is paired

WEARABLE SYNC (if applicable):
  - Wearable → Bluetooth → phone → SMS to Twilio number
  - Format: "HR:88 SpO2:97 ts:1715286000"
  - Server polls for vitals SMS during active session
  - LLM tool: read_vitals_sms() → returns latest reading if present
  - In demo: teammate manually sends this SMS mid-call to simulate

BP CUFF SELF-REPORT:
  - If Maria has a cuff: ask her to take a reading, wait, log it
  - If Maria does not have a cuff:
    - Lily switches to symptom-cluster triage
    - Structured questions: headache (location, severity, duration),
      visual changes, hand/face swelling (new or changed), epigastric
      pain, fetal movement (if applicable), dizziness
    - Symptom cluster feeds rules engine directly
    - Lily never pretends to know the BP; she escalates on the
      symptom pattern because she cannot confirm the BP
    - In pitch: frame this as "Lily escalates on uncertainty, not
      only on confirmed numbers. That's the safer design."

MEMORY ENRICHMENT (runs throughout every call):
  Every piece of information Maria shares gets logged, structured:
  - Medical: symptoms, vitals, medications mentioned, test results
    referenced, what a doctor told her
  - Equipment: gained or lost a cuff? Got a wearable?
  - Life context: support system changes, housing, transportation,
    work situation, partner situation, childcare
  - Emotional: mood, sleep, anxiety level, expressed concerns
  - Appointments: upcoming, past, what happened at them
  - Standing orders: any new authorizations from doctors
  - Follow-up flags: things Lily should raise next call

At end of call, Lily generates a call summary (internal):
  - What was discussed
  - What tier the case reached
  - Any flags for next call
  - Next scheduled proactive check-in
  - Relevant standing orders checked/used

--- CLASSIFICATION ---

After gathering sufficient information, LLM calls:
  classify_case(symptoms=[], vitals={}, history={}, flags=[])

This is a Python rules function. It does not use the LLM to decide.
It returns: {tier: "handle"|"hand_up"|"hand_off", reason: str,
             uncertainty: bool, next_action: str}

See Section 4 for full trigger conditions.

--- POST-CLASSIFICATION ACTIONS ---

See Section 4 for full tier-specific action sequences.

--- BETWEEN CALLS: PROACTIVE CHECK-INS ---

Scheduled proactive calls are initiated by the server, not Maria:

During pregnancy (adjust based on when she first enrolled):
  - Weekly check-in: brief, conversational, mood + physical update
  - Appointment follow-up: 24hrs after any appointment she mentioned

Postpartum schedule:
  - Day 3 postpartum: emotional check-in, warning signs of hemorrhage,
    baby feeding, sleep
  - Week 1: EPDS screening woven in conversationally
  - Week 2: EPDS again, physical recovery, warning signs of infection
    and DVT, emotional support
  - Week 6: Full check-in (mirrors postpartum visit topics)
  - Month 3, 6, 9, 12: Mood, physical recovery, parenting support,
    resource navigation

Each proactive call:
  - References last conversation memory
  - Builds on the relationship, does not reset it
  - Logs to record, updates follow-up flags
  - Maria can redirect it or end it anytime

--- THE DOCTOR DASHBOARD ---

Volunteer physician signup:
  - Doctors sign up via web form: name, NPI (for verification),
    specialty, state license, availability windows
  - Hackathon v1: one hardcoded volunteer "Dr. Chen" for demo
  - Pitch framing: real v2 integrates NPPES public NPI registry

Dashboard view:
  - Queue of pending cases (mid-tier hand-ups)
  - Each card shows:
    * Patient first name + gestational/postpartum stage
    * Vitals (numerical and/or symptom cluster)
    * ACOG warning signs flagged with timestamps
    * Lily's tentative recommendation
    * Specific question (forced-choice format)
    * Time remaining before auto-escalation
  - Three action buttons:
    [Approve Lily's recommendation]
    [Escalate to L&D immediately]
    [Add note and send back to Lily]
  - One free-text field for the note
  - That's it. No other UI. Designed for 15-second decisions.

Standing orders (proactive feature):
  - Doctors can write patient-specific protocols during quiet time
  - Format: "If [condition], patient may [intervention]"
  - Examples: "If nausea in first trimester: B6 25mg + Unisom 12.5mg
    as needed" / "Cleared for 30-min walks daily"
  - These appear in Lily's context for that patient
  - Lily surfaces them when relevant, never invents them

After doctor responds:
  - Decision posted to server
  - Lily calls Maria within 2 minutes
  - Uses doctor's name: "I just heard back from Dr. Chen..."
  - If escalation: conferences in facility, delivers SBAR,
    texts emergency contact, texts Maria visit summary + address

SECTION 6: TECHNICAL STACK
----------------------------------------------------------------

TELEPHONY
  - Twilio Voice (programmable voice, media streams)
  - Twilio SMS (vitals ingest, patient summaries, emergency contact)
  - Twilio Conference (three-way calling for warm handoffs)
  - Twilio programmable outbound calls (proactive check-ins,
    callbacks after doctor review)

SPEECH TO TEXT
  - Deepgram streaming API (preferred: lowest TTFT, good accuracy)
  - Fallback: Whisper streaming
  - Confidence scores on transcription — low confidence on a
    critical symptom word triggers Lily to confirm:
    "I want to make sure I heard you right — did you say [word]?"

LLM
  - claude-sonnet-4-20250514 (low TTFT, strong tool-calling)
  - System prompt defines Lily's personality, capabilities, limits,
    and available tools
  - Tool-calling handles all structured actions (memory write,
    classification call, vitals ingest, etc.)
  - LLM is stateless between turns — full conversation history
    + patient context is passed on every call

TEXT TO SPEECH
  - ElevenLabs Flash (lowest latency, streaming)
  - Alternatively: Cartesia
  - Optimize for latency over voice quality in v1
  - One consistent voice for Lily across all calls
  - Language: English for v1. Spanish for pitch as immediate next step.

RULES ENGINE
  - Plain Python function: classify_case()
  - ~50-80 lines total
  - Accepts: symptom list, vitals dict, history flags
  - Returns: tier, reason, uncertainty bool, next action
  - Based on ACOG Urgent Maternal Warning Signs (cite by name)
  - Numerical thresholds + symptom cluster thresholds
  - Tested independently of the LLM pipeline
  - No ML, no inference — deterministic, auditable

DATABASE / KNOWLEDGE GRAPH
  - SQLite for hackathon (swap to Postgres for production)
  - Schema (minimum):
    * patients: id, phone, name, due_date, baby_dob, language,
      emergency_contact_name, emergency_contact_phone,
      has_bp_cuff, has_wearable, created_at
    * conversations: id, patient_id, start_time, end_time,
      tier_reached, summary, flags_for_next_call
    * symptoms_log: id, conversation_id, symptom, value,
      source (self_report|sms_vitals|wearable), timestamp
    * vitals_log: id, patient_id, bp_systolic, bp_diastolic,
      hr, spo2, source, timestamp
    * standing_orders: id, patient_id, doctor_id, condition,
      intervention, created_at, active
    * doctor_queue: id, patient_id, conversation_id, case_packet,
      status (pending|claimed|responded|escalated), 
      response, created_at, responded_at
    * doctors: id, name, npi, specialty, active

BACKEND
  - Python + FastAPI
  - WebSocket or HTTP streaming for Twilio media streams
  - Async throughout (latency-critical)
  - Twilio webhooks: incoming call, SMS received

DOCTOR DASHBOARD
  - React frontend
  - Single view: queue of case cards
  - Polling every 15 seconds for new cases (or WebSocket push)
  - Three-button action UI per card
  - Countdown timer visible on each card
  - Separate simple form for standing order creation

HOSTING (for hackathon weekend)
  - ngrok for local tunnel to Twilio webhooks
  - Or: fly.io / Railway free tier for quick deploy

SMS VITALS INGEST
  - Twilio SMS webhook fires on any incoming SMS to the Lily number
  - Server checks: is this from a known patient's number?
  - Is there an active session for this patient?
  - If yes: parse vitals format (HR:X SpO2:X BP:X/X ts:X)
  - Push parsed vitals to active session context
  - LLM tool read_vitals_sms() retrieves on next turn

SECTION 8: THE DEMO SCRIPT (90 SECONDS)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Person A: plays Maria (on phone, calling the Lily number)
Person B: runs the doctor dashboard on a laptop visible to judges
Person C: runs the server, monitors logs (invisible to judges)
Presenter: narrates one sentence before the call starts

SETUP (10 seconds):
  "Maria is 32 weeks pregnant in rural Mississippi. The nearest OB
  closed in 2018. She's had a headache for two hours and her hands
  feel puffy. She's not sure if it's worth the 90-minute drive.
  She calls the number a doula gave her."

THE CALL (60 seconds):
  - Maria calls. Lily greets her by name (she's called before).
  - Lily asks what's going on. Maria describes the headache, swelling.
  - Lily asks warm follow-up questions. Maria mentions no visual changes.
  - Lily asks: "Do you have your cuff handy?" Maria takes a reading:
    "148 over 94."
  - Mid-call: Person C sends the wearable vitals SMS (HR:102).
    Lily says: "I also see your heart rate is a little elevated from
    your device — that's useful."
  - Lily says: "Maria, I want a doctor to look at this before I give
    you a recommendation. I'll call you back within 20 minutes."

DOCTOR DASHBOARD (15 seconds):
  - Person B turns laptop to judges.
  - Case card has appeared: Maria's packet, the specific question,
    20-minute timer counting down.
  - Person B taps [Escalate to L&D immediately].

CALLBACK (15 seconds):
  - Lily calls Maria back. Same voice.
  - "Maria, I heard back from Dr. Chen. She wants you to go to
    Mercy Regional tonight. I'm texting you and your sister the
    address right now. They know you're coming."
  - Done.

CLOSE (5 seconds):
  "No app. No data plan. Any phone. That was Lily."

SECTION 10: THE PITCH (4 MINUTES)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Minute 1: The problem. One number. One face.
  Open with a real composite story, say it's composite:
  "In April 2023, a woman we'll call Maria gave birth in rural
  Mississippi. Three days later, at home, she had a seizure from
  postpartum eclampsia. The nearest OB hospital had closed in 2018.
  The ambulance took 47 minutes. She didn't make it."
  Then the stat: 22 per 100,000. Triple peer nations. And the stat
  that does the real damage: Black women at nearly 3 times the rate.
  A gap that holds even at the highest income and education levels.
  This is not a healthcare gap. It is a justice gap.

Minute 2: The solution. What Lily is.
  "We built a phone number. Not an app — a phone number.
  Any phone. No data. No English required. Maria dials.
  Lily answers. She already knows Maria — she's been talking
  to her since week 12. She knows whether Maria has a BP cuff.
  She knows the baby's name. She knows the headache was worse
  on Tuesday. Because Lily remembers everything."

Minute 3: The demo. Let it speak.
  Run the demo from Section 8. No narration needed during the call.
  The demo narrates itself.

Minute 4: Why it's safe. What's next.
  "Lily doesn't diagnose. She pattern-matches ACOG's published
  warning signs. Every escalation decision goes through a rules
  engine any clinician can read. And when she's uncertain, she
  doesn't guess — she routes to a human, every time, within
  20 minutes, or she auto-escalates. The human is always in the loop.
  Next: Spanish, which covers the Rio Grande Valley, California's
  Central Valley, Florida's interior. Then real wearable pairing.
  Then a pilot with a rural maternal health organization."
  
  Close: "Geography should not determine survival.
  Lily is a phone number. Any phone. Any language. Free.
  For the mothers America has decided it can lose."

----------------------------------------------------------------

On diagnosis:
  "Lily does not diagnose. She identifies symptom patterns that
  match ACOG's published Urgent Maternal Warning Signs and routes
  to the appropriate human or emergency service. The word 'diagnosis'
  does not appear in our codebase."

On HIPAA:
  "Lily is designed for HIPAA compliance — encrypted storage, 
  minimum necessary data collection, explicit verbal consent at 
  registration, no data shared with insurance or immigration. 
  We are not certified. We would pursue certification as the
  first step before any real deployment."

On AI judgment:
  "The LLM handles conversation. The rules engine handles
  triage classification. They do not share that responsibility.
  The rules engine is deterministic Python, auditable by any
  clinician, grounded in ACOG guidelines. It cannot be overridden
  by the LLM."

On the companion relationship:
  "Maria can pause or stop her relationship with Lily at any time,
  verbally, in any call. Her data is hers — she receives a full
  record after every call by SMS. We do not share her data with
  employers, insurers, ICE, or any government agency. We tell her
  this at registration and periodically throughout the relationship."

On replacing clinicians:
  "Lily does not replace a clinician. She is the thing that gets
  Maria to a clinician. In a maternity desert, that gap — between
  a symptom at 2am and a clinician who can act on it — is where
  people die. Lily closes the gap. She does not replace what's
  on the other side of it."
